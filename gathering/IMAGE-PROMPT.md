# Final image prompt and production

Tool: installed `mcp__image__generate_image`, through the imagegen skill. One generation, no CLI/API fallback. The tool produced actual RGB PNG bytes at the named output path. The prompt requested 1024 square; the tool returned 1254 square. The motif was inspected before being isolated and placed on the ancestor wall. Only the final wall aliases and the required JPEG remain as images in this workspace's delivered folders.

Exact generation prompt:

> A single small prehistoric earth-pigment painting motif, intended to be composited as an addition onto an existing warm cave wall. Use an entirely plain pure white background so the pigment can be isolated, no stone background in this asset. In the center only: two side-by-side simple heavy-lidded Pepe-the-frog eyes, painted with uneven charcoal black and rusty red ochre, looking down toward one small golden yellow-ochre oval pebble. Each eye has a small black pupil and one thick drooping curved upper lid; a few worn burnt-stick marks. The two eyes and the pebble are encircled together by one incomplete, hand-drawn red ochre loop. Primitive worn cave-painting pigments, irregular thumb-dabbed texture, no smooth vector or digital icon style. Flat pigment with no drop shadows and no 3D depth. Make the motif occupy the central half of the square, with plenty of pure white surrounding it. No full frog, no hands, no digits, no handprints, no letters, numbers, text, signatures, logos, watermarks, borders or frames, no other objects.

The edit target was the exact downloaded Gathering 02 PNG, verified against its record's artifact SHA-256. Generation supplied only the new motif; it was not trusted to redraw the ancestor wall. White background pixels were discarded. Earth-pigment colors were area-resampled and blended over the existing rock within x=300..499, y=760..939. The original rock's lightness influences pigment shading. All original marks and every pixel outside the addition rectangle were retained. Generated motif and final wall hashes are recorded in `visual-checks.json`.
